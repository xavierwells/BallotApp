# Product positioning and incumbent validation

## Honest category statement

BallotApp is not the first personalized online voter guide. [VOTE411](https://www.vote411.org/) already
offers address-based ballot discovery, candidate and ballot-question
information, polling-place and election logistics, candidate answers in their
own words, and English/Spanish access. Ballotpedia, BallotReady, Vote Smart,
and other products also serve parts of this category.

The project must not claim that “no one does this.” Its working hypothesis is
that a locally operated, provenance-visible, privacy-minimizing civic-data
platform can fill verified local coverage gaps and make the same published
evidence reusable through documented APIs.

## Differentiation hypotheses—not established facts

1. **Visible auditability.** Public claims should expose their source and
   verification date, while the internal record preserves immutable documents,
   checksums, reviews, corrections, and conflicting evidence.
2. **Privacy-minimizing resolution.** BallotApp does not retain a submitted
   address or browser coordinate. This differs from [VOTE411's currently
   published voter-guide privacy policy](https://onyourballot.vote411.org/privacy-policy.do), which says an optional street address
   is stored to construct a customized ballot. This comparison must be
   rechecked if either policy changes.
3. **Reusable local infrastructure.** The software is intended to be
   self-hostable and to expose appropriately licensed published civic data
   through documented APIs. Architecture alone does not establish that all
   source content can legally be redistributed.
4. **Hyperlocal depth.** Copperas Cove may have local races or questions that
   larger guides cover late, thinly, or not at all. This remains a hypothesis
   until a dated coverage audit demonstrates it.

These are not reasons to disparage or duplicate VOTE411. Partnership, links,
and complementary coverage should be preferred when they better serve voters.

## Copperas Cove coverage audit

Run the audit twice: once when official ballots become available and again
14–21 days before Election Day. A guide that says information will be published
closer to Election Day cannot fairly be classified as missing months earlier.

The auditor may enter an address directly into a competing guide but must not
record the address in BallotApp, screenshots, tickets, analytics, or audit
notes. Prefer a consenting tester's own address; do not use another person's
residential address. Record only aggregate coverage observations.

For each product, record:

- audit date and election;
- whether exact ballot discovery succeeds;
- whether Copperas Cove municipal, CCISD, county, precinct, state, and federal
  contests appear;
- whether each candidate and proposition has substantive information;
- source visibility, verification dates, correction path, languages, and
  accessibility observations;
- whether content can be linked, embedded, exported, or reused under stated
  terms.

Classify each expected contest as `complete`, `listed but thin`, `missing`, or
`not yet published`, with an official ballot citation establishing what should
exist. Never infer a gap merely from search-engine indexing.

## Product checkpoint

Before expanding beyond the Copperas Cove pilot, summarize the audit and choose
one evidence-backed position:

- **local coverage product** where a documented gap exists;
- **open civic-data infrastructure** for a committed newsroom, nonprofit, or
  public partner;
- **complementary provenance layer** that links to incumbent guides; or
- **stop/pivot** if the pilot adds no meaningful voter or partner value.

Success is not “we built a more rigorous resolver.” Success is demonstrating
that voters or a committed operator receive useful coverage, trust, privacy, or
reuse value they could not obtain as effectively from existing services.
